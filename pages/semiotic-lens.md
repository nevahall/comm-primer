# Meaning and Culture

Semiotics adds a question that transmission models leave in the background: how does a phenomenon become meaningful? A word, image, interface button, accent, or silence is a sign because people interpret it through shared codes. Meaning is made through relationships among a sign, what it refers to, and the cultural habits that make the connection recognizable.

<section class="panel" aria-labelledby="semiotic-question">
  <h2 id="semiotic-question">The semiotic question</h2>
  <p>Ask what a message represents, which codes it relies on, and what identities or values it makes ordinary. The same color, phrase, or image can signify trust in one setting and danger in another because meaning is cultural rather than fixed inside the object.</p>
</section>

<section class="panel" aria-labelledby="ai-signs">
  <h2 id="ai-signs">A contemporary application</h2>
  <p>AI-generated language and images draw on existing signs and conventions, then present them in new combinations. Reading those outputs semiotically means examining whose references are centered, whose meanings become legible, and what the system makes difficult to imagine.</p>
</section>
