# Interpretation and Power

Another way to understand AI-written emails is through encoding and decoding, a communication theory developed by Stuart Hall. Encoding is the process of creating a message with a certain meaning, while decoding is how someone interprets that message. In simple terms, encoding is what the sender is trying to say, and decoding is what the receiver thinks the sender means. These two meanings are not always the same because people understand messages based on their own experiences, feelings, and beliefs.

For example, imagine an employee uses AI to write an email reminding a coworker about an upcoming deadline. The employee wants the message to sound professional and helpful. However, AI might write it in a way that sounds demanding or impatient. When the coworker reads the email, they might feel like they are being criticized instead of reminded. The employee had good intentions, but the coworker understood the message differently. This shows how a message can be interpreted in a way the sender did not expect.
People can also have different reactions to the exact same message. Hall explained that people might accept a message as intended, agree with only part of it, or reject the meaning altogether. These are called dominant, negotiated, and oppositional readings. For example, one employee might read an AI-written reminder as helpful, while another might see it as unnecessary criticism. Neither person is necessarily reading the words incorrectly. They are simply understanding the message based on their own experiences and expectations.

AI also raises questions about who has control over communication. Employees might think they are completely controlling their emails because they tell AI what to write. However, AI is choosing the specific language used in the final message. For example, someone might feel that their normal writing style is not professional enough and constantly ask AI to make their emails sound more formal. Over time, they might begin relying on AI instead of using their own voice. Although AI can help people communicate more confidently, it can also change how they express themselves. This is why employees should make sure the final email actually reflects what they want to say.
