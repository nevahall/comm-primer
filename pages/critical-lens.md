# Interpretation and Power

Stuart Hall's encoding/decoding model explains why producing a message does not guarantee control over its meaning. Institutions encode preferred meanings through language, images, genres, technologies, and assumptions about audiences. Viewers then decode those messages from their own social positions and may accept, negotiate, or oppose the preferred reading.

<section class="panel" aria-labelledby="hall-readings">
  <h2 id="hall-readings">Three positions</h2>
  <p>A dominant-hegemonic reading accepts the intended framework. A negotiated reading accepts some of it while adapting it to lived experience. An oppositional reading understands the preferred meaning but rejects its terms. These are positions shaped by context, history, and power.</p>
</section>

<section class="panel" aria-labelledby="power-question">
  <h2 id="power-question">The power question</h2>
  <p>Ask who had the resources to encode the message, whose interpretation is treated as authoritative, and what happens when people resist it. Platforms and AI systems can influence all three stages by selecting what is visible, classifying users, and rewarding some readings with reach.</p>
</section>
